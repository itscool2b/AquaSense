Step 9 of 9

Put it in the water

Permits before the mooring. This kit has not been in a lake. You are the operator.

Permission

Florida: private water is between you and the landowner. Public water and preserves need the manager. Aids to navigation are regulated — see FWC 68D-23 and, if the Coast Guard would treat it as a PATON, their PATON rules. Hull yellow or white with reflective tape. Do not paint it like a channel marker.

Mooring

Scope for wind and tide. Keep probes off the bottom. Note the GPS fix on first wake so you can find it again.

Pull the EC probe between deployments. DFR0300-H is lab-grade: DFRobot says it is not for long-term immersion. This kit is a buoy, not a permission to leave that probe in a lake for a season. Rinse with distilled water, recap, store wet-capped as the wiki says.

What “healthy” last-seen looks like

  • A new row about every 15 minutes (900 s) while the battery is up.
  • Battery not collapsing overnight if the 10 W panel saw sun the day before.
  • DO below 2 mg/L is an ecological hypoxia rule of thumb on the dashboard — not an FDEP lab limit.
  • GitHub Pages gauges are fixture data labeled simulated. Trust only the site you host.
Do not publish kit readings as regulatory data. Do not claim a named preserve deployment without written permission.
Checkpoint. You have written permission, a mooring plan, and a dashboard last-seen you understand. The project stops being a kit and starts being your station.
