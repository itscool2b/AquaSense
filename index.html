AquaSense

A solar buoy that sits on the water, measures a few stats, uses cellular internet to send them to a website you host.

Build the kit Bill of materials

We do not run a cloud. GitHub Pages is documentation and a simulated dashboard. Live numbers come from docker compose up on a machine you control.

Generated photoreal of the BOB-style hull — not a field photo. Parts count: labeled isometric and exploded SVG.

What it measures

One HTTPS POST every 15 minutes. No turbidity — the cheap analog probe is not waterproof and is not NTU.

Temperature

Waterproof DS18B20 (DFRobot KIT0021). Digital 1-Wire on GPIO 18.

pH

SEN0169-V2 industrial analog transmitter. 0–3.0 V into GPIO 32. Two-point buffers on the desk.

Conductivity → salinity

DFR0300-H K=10, 10–100 mS/cm. PSS-78 at atmospheric pressure (UNESCO 44 / Fofonoff & Millard 1983). Lab-grade probe — DFRobot: not for long-term immersion.

Dissolved oxygen

SEN0237-A galvanic. Two-point millivolts in NVS; % saturation from Benson & Krause 1984. Uncalibrated POSTs send null, not a fake 0–20 scale.

Depth

SparkFun MS5803-14BA. Gauge pressure × 0.01 ≈ metres of water.

Where / power

L76K GPS, LTE RSSI, battery millivolts. The board sleeps between wakes so a 10 W panel and a cell last overnight.

Your buoy, your website

Wake every 15 minutes. CSV on the SD card if LTE fails. The ingest URL is yours.

The hull

24″ PVC pipe square (Levin BOB float), Pelican 1120, 10 W panel on the lid, five sensors in a cage. Gravity cables — no custom PCB.

Exactly five sensors: pH, EC, DO, DS18B20, MS5803. Solar sits on the case lid, not under a second box.

Cost, honestly

A YSI EXO2 sonde lists around $7,300–$8,450 before sensors (YSI EXO2, Fondriest). A NexSens-class data buoy is another few thousand. This kit’s CSV sums to about $866 of street prices — check the linked pages; they move.

AquaSense has not been in a lake. Treat every number as a kit you calibrate, not a certified station.

Nine steps

  1. Buy the list — every row has a Buy button.
  2. Stand up your website — Docker, then the simulator.
  3. Flash the board — USB-A to USB-C, PlatformIO.
  4. Config the SD card — your ingest URL and token.
  5. Calibrate on the desk — buffers, NaOH safety first.
  6. Wire the box — colors match the drawing.
  7. Build the hull — PVC, glands, solar, cage.
  8. Bucket test — one real POST to localhost.
  9. Put it in the water — permits, mooring, last-seen.
